# CTA Writing

**Type:** Framework Note

---

## Core Idea

The Call to Action (CTA) is the final instruction you give the reader. It should be specific, low-friction, and benefit-led. A weak CTA wastes everything that came before it.

---

## CTA Anatomy

```
[Action Verb] + [What They Get] + [Friction Reducer]
```

| Part | Weak Version | Strong Version |
|---|---|---|
| Action Verb | "Click here" | "Start", "Get", "Try", "See", "Unlock" |
| What They Get | "Learn more" | "your free copy audit" |
| Friction Reducer | — | "no card needed", "in 60 seconds", "free forever" |

**Example:**
> "Get your free copy audit — no card needed."

---

## CTA Types by Goal

| Goal | CTA Examples |
|---|---|
| Free trial | "Start free", "Try it free for 14 days" |
| Lead gen | "Get the free guide", "Book a 15-min call" |
| Purchase | "Shop now", "Grab yours before it's gone" |
| Content | "Read the full breakdown", "Watch how it works" |
| App install | "Download free", "Get the app" |

---

## CTA Placement Rules

- **One CTA per ad** — multiple CTAs split focus and lower conversion
- **Mirror the hook** — if the hook promises something, the CTA delivers it
- **Match platform norms** — LinkedIn tolerates "Connect with us," TikTok prefers "Link in bio" or overlay text
- **Use urgency sparingly** — "Today only" works once; overuse destroys trust

---

## Common CTA Mistakes

- "Click here" — tells the reader nothing
- "Learn more" — vague, zero benefit
- "Submit" on forms — makes the action feel like work
- Multiple CTAs in one ad — creates decision paralysis
- CTA that doesn't match the landing page destination
