# AIDA Framework

**Type:** Framework Note  
**Links:** [[Copywriting Fundamentals]] · [[PAS Framework]] · [[Hook Writing]] · [[CTA Writing]] · [[Performance Ads]]

---

## Core Idea

AIDA is one of the oldest and most reliable copywriting frameworks. It maps the psychological journey a reader takes from first seeing an ad to taking action.

**A — Attention → I — Interest → D — Desire → A — Action**

---

## Breakdown

### A — Attention
Stop the scroll. The first line, headline, or visual must interrupt the reader's pattern.

> *"You're losing £3,000/month to bad ad copy."*

### I — Interest
Build curiosity or relevance. Show the reader this is for them.

> *"Most brands write copy that explains what they do — not what the customer gets. Here's the difference."*

### D — Desire
Make them want the outcome. Paint the picture of life after the solution.

> *"Imagine launching campaigns in 10 minutes with copy that's already been tested against 10,000 variants."*

### A — Action
One clear, low-friction step.

> *"Try it free — no card needed."*

---

## When to Use AIDA

- Long-form ad copy (Facebook feed, landing pages)
- Email sequences
- Video scripts where there's time to build the arc
- Any context where you have more than ~50 words

---

## AIDA vs PAS

| AIDA | PAS |
|---|---|
| Attention-first | Problem-first |
| Builds aspiration | Amplifies pain |
| Better for cold audiences | Better for warm / pain-aware audiences |
| Longer format | Shorter, punchier |
