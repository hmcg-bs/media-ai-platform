# Hook Writing

**Type:** Framework Note  
**Links:** [[Copywriting Fundamentals]] · [[AIDA Framework]] · [[PAS Framework]] · [[Performance Ads]] · [[Organic Ads]] · [[CTA Writing]]

---

## Core Idea

The hook is the first thing a reader sees. On most platforms, it determines whether they stop or scroll. A strong hook doesn't just grab attention — it creates a reason to keep reading.

---

## Hook Categories

### 1. The Provocative Statement
Makes a bold or counterintuitive claim that demands a reaction.
> *"Cold email is dead. Here's what's replacing it."*
> *"Most landing pages convert at 1%. This one converts at 11%."*

### 2. The Direct Question
Speaks directly to a pain or desire.
> *"Still writing all your ad copy by hand?"*
> *"What if your next campaign was done in 10 minutes?"*

### 3. The Specific Number
Specificity creates credibility and curiosity.
> *"47 ad hooks that increased CTR by 3x."*
> *"We tested 200 headlines. Here's what won."*

### 4. The Story Open
Drops the reader into a moment without explaining it.
> *"Last Tuesday, a client's ROAS doubled overnight. This is what changed."*

### 5. The Relatable Problem
Names a frustration so precisely the reader feels seen.
> *"You spend 3 hours writing ad copy. It gets 0 clicks. Again."*

### 6. The Contrast Hook
Before vs. after in a single sentence.
> *"From £1.20 CPC to £0.34 — same audience, different copy."*

---

## Platform-Specific Hook Rules

| Platform | Hook Rule |
|---|---|
| TikTok / Reels | First 1–2 seconds must create visual + text hook together |
| Meta Feed | First line of caption carries the hook (truncated at ~125 chars) |
| Google Search | Headline must match search intent — hook = relevance |
| Email | Subject line is the hook — 6–9 words sweet spot |
| LinkedIn | First line before "see more" break is the hook |

---

## Hook Testing Framework

1. Write 5–10 hook variants per campaign
2. Test 2–3 at a time in small budget splits
3. Kill losers after 500+ impressions
4. Double down on winners, then write hooks in the same style
